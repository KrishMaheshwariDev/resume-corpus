## Reader objective

A recruiter should see a C++/software-engineering intern or fresher candidate with concrete projects, not someone overstating professional experience.

## Strong first-page signals

- C++20, CMake, OpenGL, data structures, debugging, and performance appear in visible project context.
- OpenChess or Black Hole Simulation leads for systems, graphics, or C++ roles.
- Get-My-Doc, LogWhisperer, or EDA Dashboarding leads only when backend, Python, SQL, or data relevance is stronger.
- Education and activities reinforce the fresher profile; no Work Experience section is fabricated.

## Credibility rules

- Keep OpenChess and Get-My-Doc marked in development.
- Treat all projects as P2 evidence.
- Attach benchmark figures to what was measured: OpenChess rendering/board representation, Black Hole Simulation TPS/FPS, or FolderSort file count.
- Keep C and Java qualified as foundational.
- Do not claim production users, employers, internships, customer impact, or leadership.

## Scan and writing rules

- Use short project titles, a technology line, and one or two substantive bullets.
- Prefer implementation nouns that invite technical follow-up: bitboards, fixed timestep, GPU rendering, schema management, file scanning, vertex arrays, and CMake.
- Use project breadth selectively; do not turn Skills into a tool inventory.
- Preserve readable 11 pt text, single-column layout, and clear section separation.

## Genuine gaps to report

- No professional employment or internship history.
- No user-confirmed GPA or work-authorization details.
- Limited evidence for production backend, cloud operations, test frameworks, and large-team delivery.
